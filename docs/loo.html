LOO and WAIC

Leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO) estimates how well a model predicts each observation when that observation is left out of the fit. It is used to compare models. CmdStanPHP computes PSIS-LOO, which approximates LOO from a single MCMC fit with Pareto-smoothed importance sampling, and WAIC. Both need a pointwise log-likelihood variable, usually log_lik, in the generated quantities block:

generated quantities {
  vector[N] log_lik;
  for (n in 1:N) {
    log_lik[n] = bernoulli_lpmf(y[n] | theta);
  }
}

PSIS-LOO

$loo = $fit->loo('log_lik');
echo $loo['elpd_loo'], ' ± ', $loo['se_elpd_loo'], "\n";
print_r($loo['diagnostics']['pareto_k']);
$waic = $fit->waic('log_lik');

loo() computes PSIS-LOO from a pointwise log-likelihood variable in generated quantities. It follows the loo package as called by CmdStanR 1.0. Relative efficiencies are skipped by default (rEff: false), because they can be slow to compute; pass rEff: true to compute them. Results contain estimates, pointwise rows, Pareto k diagnostics, and k_threshold. Inspect observations whose Pareto k exceeds the threshold.

Moment matching

$loo = $fit->loo('log_lik', momentMatch: true);
foreach ($loo['moment_match']['observations'] as $o) {
    echo $o['observation'], ': k ', $o['pareto_k_initial'], ' -> ', $o['pareto_k'], ' (', $o['stop'], ")\n";
}

momentMatch: true applies importance-weighted moment matching (Paananen et al., 2021) to each observation whose Pareto k exceeds the threshold, as loo::loo_moment_match() does for CmdStanR. It needs model methods (FFI) and the posterior-draw kernels of the phit extension: Phit\draws_moments(), Phit\draws_covariance_map(), and Phit\draws_affine(). CmdStanPHP checks for these functions by name and throws a RuntimeException naming any that are missing. The options max_iters (default 30), k_threshold (default min(1 - 1/log10(S), 0.7)), split (default true), and cov (default true) have loo’s meanings. The covariance step runs only when there are at least ten draws per parameter.

The draws are the fit’s post-warmup draws on the unconstrained scale, in chain-major order, so row s of the unconstrained draws, the log-likelihood draws, and the importance weights all refer to the same draw. Each iteration tries a shift to the weighted mean, a per-parameter rescaling, and a covariance map, and keeps the first one that lowers Pareto k. The proposal density of a transformed draw T(x) is p(x)/|det T|, where p is logProb() with jacobian: true and |det T| comes from the kernel’s log_abs_det. The log-likelihood of a transformed draw is recomputed with constrainVariables(). The split step moves half of the draws by the accumulated transformation and weights all draws against the mixture of the posterior and the transformed posterior.

The result keeps the shape of loo() and updates pointwise, diagnostics, estimates, the flat estimate keys, and warnings, as loo does. influence_pareto_k keeps its PSIS value. The added moment_match key lists each matched observation with its initial and final Pareto k, the full-posterior k (pareto_kf), the accepted transformations, their total log determinant, whether the split was used, and why the iterations ended. stop is one of these values:

  • k_below_threshold: Pareto k reached the threshold.
  • no_improvement: no transformation lowered Pareto k.
  • max_iters: the iteration limit was reached.
  • covariance_not_positive_definite: a covariance could not be factorized, or the weighted covariance had fewer than two effective draws.
  • singular_transform or nonfinite_transform: an affine map was numerically singular or produced nonfinite values.
  • model_error: the model threw while evaluating transformed draws.
  • invalid_log_ratios: the transformed importance ratios could not be Pareto smoothed.

In the last five cases the observation keeps its last accepted transformation, message records the failure, and a warning counts the affected observations. Covariances are never regularized. A failed weighted covariance instead uses loo’s identity linear map, retaining the mean shift and recording the reason in covariance_fallbacks. This normally repeats the rejected mean-shift step and ends with no_improvement. A split that fails falls back to the unsplit estimate and is recorded in message.

The split step differs from loo 2.10.1 and from development builds before stan-dev/loo#405 (merged on 2026-10-13) in two ways. That pull request made the same two split corrections in loo, and the compatibility suite checks that a corrected loo’s split matches CmdStanPHP on its test designs. CmdStanPHP uses the exact inverse T⁻¹(y) = A⁻¹(y − m − t) + m of the accumulated map T(x) = A(x − m) + m + t. Accepted scale and covariance matrices are multiplied in acceptance order, so noncommuting transformations retain their original effect. loo uses A⁻¹(y − m) + m − t, which is not the inverse once a shift is combined with scaling or a covariance map, so loo evaluates part of the mixture density at the wrong points. CmdStanPHP also takes the split relative efficiency as the minimum over both halves of the draws, where loo uses the second half twice. Relative efficiencies are computed from exp(log_lik − max). loo exponentiates unshifted values, and posterior then reports NA when every value is tiny. Without the split, and with the split when only shifts are accepted, elpd_loo and Pareto k match CmdStanR.

CmdStan\MomentMatch::loo() runs the same algorithm on any unconstrained draws with caller-supplied logProbUpars and logLikIUpars callbacks. CmdStan\DrawKernels wraps the three kernels.
